## 1. Purpose & Bayesian Framing

This artifact establishes historical **base rates** for the recurring dynamics of EP mid-term
politics, so that the year-ahead forecasts elsewhere in this set rest on calibrated priors rather
than narrative intuition. For each forecast question we state a **prior** (the historical base rate),
the **evidence** from the current EP10 configuration and 2026 adopted texts, and a **posterior**
(the revised probability, expressed in WEP bands). Confidence labels 🟢/🟡/🔴 mark the strength of
the underlying record. The discipline is Bayesian in spirit: *posterior ∝ prior × likelihood*.

## 2. EP Term Comparatives — Where EP10's Mid-Term Sits

| EP term | Mid-term year | Dominant dynamic | Legislative outcome |
|---------|---------------|------------------|---------------------|
| EP6 | 2005 | Barroso I consolidation | Productive, programmatic |
| EP7 | 2011 | Eurozone crisis (ESM, Six-Pack) | High output, reactive |
| EP8 | 2015 | Refugee crisis + Brexit run-up | Mixed, crisis-consumed |
| EP9 | 2020 | COVID + Green Deal pipeline | Very high output |
| **EP10** | **2026–27** | **Competitiveness, defence, MFF, fragmentation** | **Forecast: high but slow** |

**Base-rate lesson (🟢 HIGH):** the mid-term (roughly year 2–3) of an EP term is historically the
**most productive legislative window** — rapporteurships are settled, inter-institutional habits are
formed, and the pipeline is in full flow. The prior therefore favours high output for EP10 in
2026-27. The new evidence that *tempers* it is record fragmentation (effective number of parties
≈ 6.6) and the fiscal squeeze (IMF core growth under 1%), which slow coalition assembly.
**Posterior:** high legislative volume but elongated timelines — *Likely* (70%). 🟡 MEDIUM.

## 3. Precedent Set 1 — MFF / Budget Fights

| Episode | Pattern | Resolution |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| MFF 2007–2013 | Protracted net-contributor vs cohesion standoff | Agreed with rebate compromises |
| EU Budget 2011 | 18-month own-resources demand by EP | Political package, late deal |
| MFF 2014–2020 | Heavy EP–Council conflict; EP gained structural role | Late agreement + flexibility |
| MFF 2021–2027 | COVID shock; RRF added *outside* the MFF; own-resources roadmap | Crisis-driven breakthrough |

**Prior:** every MFF negotiation in EU history has eventually resolved, but only after a
deadline-driven, political-level breakthrough, typically with a new flexibility or own-resources
sweetener (base rate of "resolved late, with sweetener" ≈ 0.9).
**Evidence (EP10):** the MFF post-2027 opens with net contributors more fiscally constrained than in
any prior round — IMF projects **France's deficit at −4.94% of GDP in 2026** and **Germany's at
−4.23% in 2027**, inverting the historic anchor. Own-resources logic (ETS2, CBAM) is therefore
stronger, but ceiling resistance is also harder.
**Posterior:** an own-resources-versus-ceiling standoff resolving late in or beyond the horizon —
*Likely* (65%). A breakthrough *within* the year-ahead window is *Unlikely* (30%). 🟡 MEDIUM.

## 4. Precedent Set 2 — Far-Right Parliamentary Consolidation

| Episode | Pattern | Lesson |
|---------|---------|--------|
| EP6 ITS group (2007) | 58-seat far-right group; collapsed in months | Early far-right groups fragile |
| EP7–EP8 | UKIP, M5S complicate coalitions; EPP–S&D grand coalition strained | Gradual mainstream erosion |
| EP9 | ID (PfE predecessor) and ECR grow substantially | Trend toward current constellation |
| EP10 (now) | PfE 84 + ECR 78 + ESN 25 = 187 nominal | Cumulative 15-year trend, not a shock |

**Prior:** far-right consolidation is a slow, multi-term structural trend; far-right groups have a
historical base rate of internal fragility and coordination friction (ITS collapse; ID/PfE
Le Pen–Orbán tensions).
**Evidence (EP10):** the three right groups hold 187 seats but remain split (PfE cohesion ~78%, ESN
~75%, ECR's Meloni-pragmatism vs harder nationalists). They cannot form a majority alone
(187, −174 short of 361); their influence is realised only when **EPP** chooses the ad-hoc right
route, which then carries just +14.
**Posterior:** continued consolidation and more frequent ad-hoc right majorities on
migration/agriculture — *Highly Likely* (80%) — but a stable, disciplined right governing bloc —
*Unlikely* (25%). 🟡 MEDIUM.

## 5. Precedent Set 3 — Digital Governance Lifecycle

| Instrument | Legislative span | Effect |
|------------|------------------|--------|
| GDPR | ~4 years to application (2018) | "Brussels effect" in 90+ jurisdictions |
| DMA | ~18 months; enforcement from 2024-25 | EP strengthened vs Council |
| DSA | enforcement ramp against gatekeepers | platform-accountability regime |
| AI Act | ~3 years; fundamental-rights provisions strengthened by EP | first enforcement milestones 2026-27 |

**Prior:** EU digital legislation takes years to enact but creates durable extraterritorial effects;
the *enforcement* phase is a distinct, less coalition-fraught challenge than legislation (base rate:
mainstream consensus holds on enforcement).
**Evidence (EP10):** 2026 adopted texts show DMA/DSA enforcement and AI-Act first milestones landing
now, backed by the centrist majority plus Greens.
**Posterior:** digital enforcement proceeds with stable mainstream backing — *Highly Likely* (85%).
🟢 HIGH.

## 6. Precedent Set 4 — Ukraine Support Continuity

| Episode | Pattern |
|---------|---------|
| EP6–EP7 | Eastern Partnership; Neighbourhood resolutions |
| 2014 | Association Agreement ratified post-Maidan |
| Post-2022 | EP becomes a major institutional advocate; unprecedented instrument scale |
| 2026 | Macro-financial loan via immobilised Russian assets |

**Prior:** EP Ukraine support has been consistent across terms and majorities; even ECR broadly
backs Ukrainian sovereignty (high base rate of passage for Ukraine instruments).
**Evidence (EP10):** the macro-financial loan's immobilised-asset framing minimises direct GNI
outlays — attractive precisely because member states are fiscally constrained (IMF France deficit
−4.94% of GDP in 2026). PfE opposes; ESN opposes; the centrist majority plus most of ECR carries it.
**Posterior:** Ukraine financing instruments pass — *Highly Likely* (80%). 🟢 HIGH.

## 7. Precedent Set 5 — Enlargement Waves & Unanimity Blocks

| Wave | Pattern | Lesson |
|------|---------|--------|
| 2004 "big bang" (10 states) | Long accession-chapter grind; political will decisive | Momentum can overcome friction |
| 2007 (Romania, Bulgaria) | Conditionality + post-accession monitoring | Monitoring tools persist |
| 2013 (Croatia) | Slow but completed | Unanimity surmountable with will |
| 2026-27 (Ukraine, Moldova, W. Balkans) | Chapter momentum vs unanimity vetoes | Geopolitics raises stakes |

**Prior:** enlargement advances in slow waves, repeatedly stalled by unanimity requirements yet
historically completed when geopolitical will is sustained (base rate of eventual accession high,
but timelines measured in years).
**Evidence (EP10):** accession-chapter momentum for Ukraine/Moldova/Western Balkans is real but
exposed to unanimity blocks (notably Hungary).
**Posterior:** visible chapter progress within the horizon — *Likely* (60%); any actual accession in
the window — *Almost No Chance* (<5%). 🟡 MEDIUM.

## 8. Precedent Set 6 — Rule-of-Law Conditionality

- **Hungary (Article 7 since 2018):** longest such process in EU history — demonstrates both
  deterrent value and limited enforcement capacity.
- **Poland (largely reversed 2023–25):** shows rule-of-law pressure plus electoral change can work.

**Prior:** conditionality tools remain active but rarely deliver swift resolution; the
budget–conditionality nexus recurs (base rate: persistent litigation, slow outcomes).
**Posterior:** rule-of-law remains a live battleground coupled to the MFF — *Almost Certain* (90%);
swift resolution — *Unlikely* (25%). 🟢 HIGH that it persists.

## 10. Baseline Assessment Entering 2026-27

The EP10 Parliament enters its mid-term having: ✅ completed institutional setup; ✅ settled
rapporteurships; ✅ tested both majorities (centrist on budget/Ukraine/digital, ad-hoc right on
migration); ✅ kept rule-of-law tools active. It has **not yet** faced its first full MFF post-2027
reading or a major institutional crisis (provisional twelfths, censure).

**Historical conclusion:** the priors point to a high-output but slow mid-term, an MFF fight that
resolves late and with an own-resources sweetener, continued far-right consolidation without a
stable right bloc, and durable Ukraine and digital-enforcement majorities. The novel variable the
historical record cannot fully price is the **inverted fiscal picture** — IMF projecting Germany
(−4.23% of GDP in 2027) running a larger deficit than Italy (−2.58%) — which has no clean precedent
and therefore widens the forecast intervals on every budget-adjacent question. 🟡 MEDIUM overall;
🟢 HIGH on the historical facts.
