## 🎯 BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)

The European Parliament enters the second half of its EP10 term (2024–2029) as a structurally stable
but politically congested institution, where the centrist grand coalition (EPP–S&D–Renew, ~401 of 720
seats) still owns the institutional and budgetary machinery, yet the EPP increasingly assembles ad-hoc
right-leaning majorities (EPP+ECR+PfE) on migration, deregulation and agriculture. Over the year ahead
the dominant battleground is fiscal: the **post-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF)** opens in
earnest, pitting net contributors against cohesion and farm beneficiaries while three large economies run
constrained budgets — IMF projects a German general-government deficit of **-3.78% of GDP in 2026**
widening to **-4.23% in 2027**, France at **-4.94% of GDP in 2026**, and Italy at **-2.82% in 2026**.
Growth offers no relief: IMF puts German real GDP growth at **0.79% in 2026** and French growth at
**0.86% in 2026**, leaving little fiscal headroom for the defence-readiness, enlargement and Ukraine-support
commitments stacking up on the agenda. It is **Likely** that the year is defined less by dramatic ruptures
than by attritional coalition arithmetic around money, trade (Mercosur) and the green-versus-competitiveness
"omnibus" deregulation drive.

**One-line judgement:** A functional but fragmented Parliament will spend the year negotiating *who pays*
for defence, enlargement and the green transition — and the centre will hold, narrowly, on the files that
matter most. 🟡 MEDIUM.

## 🌍 Three Forces, One Equilibrium

A force-field reading of the year ahead locates the equilibrium in attrition rather than rupture. The
**driving forces** — defence-readiness urgency, enlargement momentum (Ukraine, Moldova, Western Balkans),
and the competitiveness/"omnibus" deregulation agenda — all push the Parliament toward bigger spending and
faster decisions. The **restraining forces** — constrained national budgets (IMF deficits above 3% of GDP
across the big three, France at **-4.94% of GDP in 2026**), farm and cohesion resistance, and Council
unanimity blocks — pull the other way. Where these balance is the year's central truth: **movement on
instruments, stalemate on headline numbers.** It is **Likely** that defence-financing instruments advance
while joint EU bonds stall; **Likely** that the MFF opens but does not close; and **Roughly Even** that
enlargement clusters open against unanimity resistance.

The far-right variable sits across all three forces. PfE (~84), ECR (~78) and ESN (~25) together command
roughly 187 seats — enough to extract concessions on migration and agriculture, not enough to govern the
institution. The partial `compare_political_groups` feed (PfE=85, ECR=81, ESN=27; parliamentaryBalance
0.61, C3) is consistent with that reading. Their leverage grows incrementally through NI defections and
national-election wins, but a legislative blocking pivot on core EU files remains **Unlikely** within the
horizon.

## 🧩 Strategic Read

The year ahead is a **money-and-trade year**, not a constitutional one. The three structural facts that
shape every vote are: (1) the EPP's 188-seat primacy without a majority, forcing perpetual dealmaking;
(2) constrained national fiscal positions — no large member state has comfortable headroom, with France
at **-4.94% of GDP (IMF, 2026)** and Germany's deficit widening to **-4.23% in 2027 (IMF)**; and (3) a
far-right bloc large enough to extract concessions on migration and agriculture but not yet large enough
to govern the institution. Expect the centre to hold on institutional and budget files (**Likely**, 🟢/🟡),
the right to win selectively on deregulation and migration (**Highly Likely**, 🟢), and the genuinely
open questions — Mercosur, MFF size, enlargement pace — to remain **Roughly Even** and unresolved as the
Parliament edges toward its 2027 mid-term and the long shadow of the 2029 election.
