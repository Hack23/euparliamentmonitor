# 📜 Historical Baseline — Run 188

**Context:** EP 10th Parliament (since June 2024) legislative output analysis

## Banking Union Legislative Chronology

The March 26 Banking Union trilogy (SRMR3/BRRD3/DGSD2) completes an arc begun in 2014:
- **2014**: BRRD1 adopted (original Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive)
- **2014**: SRMR1 adopted (establishing the Single Resolution Board)
- **2015**: DGSD1 fully transposed across member states
- **2018-2020**: BRRD2 amendment package (TLAC/MREL alignment with FSB standards)
- **2021-2023**: Commission SRMR2/BRRD3/DGSD2 proposal package
- **2025**: EP ECON committee vote on Banking Union trilogy
- **2026-03-26**: EP plenary adoption (Runs 179-188 series confirming)
- **Next**: Council ratification (estimated May-July 2026), national transposition (24-30 months)

**Historical precedent significance**: BRRD1 adoption in 2014 was credited with reducing EU sovereign-bank loop stress (ECB Financial Stability Review 2015). BRRD3+SRMR3+DGSD2 addresses remaining gaps in resolution financing mechanisms that were exposed in 2023 Credit Suisse AT1 wipeout and US regional bank failures.

## Anti-Corruption Directive Historical Gap

No EU-wide binding anti-corruption legislative standard existed before TA-10-2026-0094. Context:
- **2003**: UNCAC (UN Convention Against Corruption) — non-binding soft law reference
- **2011-2023**: Commission annual anti-corruption reports (monitoring, not legislative)
- **2022**: Rule of Law Conditionality Regulation enforcement against Hungary/Poland (adjacent mechanism)
- **2023-0135**: Commission proposal for Combating Corruption Directive
- **2026-03-26**: EP adoption — first binding EU standard

**Historical significance**: The 23-year gap between UNCAC and first binding EU anti-corruption law reflects the difficulty of harmonizing criminal law at EU level (subsidiarity, national sovereignty). Procedure 2023-0135 moved from proposal to adoption in under 3 years — unusually fast for criminal law harmonization.

## US-EU Trade Dispute Historical Pattern

Section 301 has been used against EU predecessors four times:
1. **1999**: US Section 301 against EU banana import regime (WTO dispute BD.DS27)
2. **2004**: US Section 301 against EU Airbus subsidies (WTO DS316)
3. **2018**: US Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs (EU counter-retaliation)
4. **2022-2024**: US IRA subsidy dispute (IRA vs EU Green Deal)

Current 2026 context: USTR has signaled interest in reviewing EU AI Act and DMA compliance with US tech companies as potential Section 301 targets. This would be the first time digital regulation was the primary Section 301 target in the EU context — a fundamentally different legal and economic dynamic from goods trade disputes.

**Historical baseline**: All four prior US-EU trade disputes required 18-36 months to resolve through WTO processes or bilateral negotiation. An April 2026 Section 301 decision would likely not be resolved before 2027-2028.
