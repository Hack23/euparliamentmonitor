**Confidence:** 🟢 HIGH | Source grade: A1 (EP adopted text) | WEP: *Almost Certain*

TA-10-2026-0183 marks the first time the EP has adopted a resolution specifically addressing AI as a *trade instrument* rather than solely as a regulatory compliance challenge. This positions the EP to demand Commission proposals on AI export controls, AI-enabled trade facilitation, and reciprocal AI standards clauses in future Free Trade Agreements. ## 2. Strategic Context

The May 2026 plenary occurs within a structurally significant period for EU foreign and technology policy:

- **AI governance race**: The EU AI Act has been in force for over a year; the EP's AI trade resolution now moves the agenda from internal market compliance to *external dimension*, asking what AI means for trade partner relations, export controls, and WTO compatibility.
- **Central Asia pivot**: Uzbekistan is the largest and most strategically significant Central Asian state; EPCA ratification cements a partnership framework that Brussels hopes will serve as a model for Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan relations.
- **Mediterranean neighbourhood pressure**: The EU-Lebanon Eurojust agreement reflects ongoing EU investment in rule-of-law infrastructure with a state undergoing post-2024 political reconstruction.
- **Fisheries portfolio active**: Back-to-back fisheries agreement ratifications (São Tomé, Cook Islands) reflect active management of EU's Blue Economy partnerships in the Atlantic and Pacific.
- **Budget 2027 preparations**: The 2027 budget draft (TA-10-2026-04-30-ANN01) adopted at end of April continues to cast a shadow over EP internal dynamics as political groups jostle over spending priorities.
