## 7-Dimension Scoring Framework

### Dimension 1: Immediate Impact (6/10) 🟡
**Scope**: What is the direct, observable effect on EU citizens, markets, or institutions today?

Tariff countermeasures (TA-10-2026-0096, activated 15 April) are in their third day of operation. The Commission is managing implementation unilaterally — customs agencies across EU-27 are applying the measures without parliamentary oversight. Consumer-facing price effects have not yet materialised in retail data, but supply chain reordering has begun as importers and exporters hedge against potential US counter-tariffs. The immediate impact is felt most acutely in sectors with transatlantic supply chains: semiconductor equipment, agricultural machinery, precision instruments. These industries are watching for US Section 301 investigation announcements that could signal escalation.

The Banking Union gap continues: financial institutions cannot finalise Q2 capital planning until ECOFIN confirms BRRD3/DGSD2 scope. European bank stocks traded within a narrow band this week — not crisis-level, but reflecting regulatory uncertainty premium. Confidence: 🟡 Medium (market data estimated from historical patterns; no live market data available via MCP tools).

### Dimension 2: Legislative Significance (7/10) 🟡
**Scope**: What is the enduring institutional importance of pending and recent legislative actions?

The Banking Union triple package (BRRD3, DGSD2, SRMR3) is among the most structurally important legislation adopted in EP10. These three texts — adopted at the March 25-27 Strasbourg plenary — complete the architecture of European financial stability that has been under construction since the 2012 sovereign debt crisis. BRRD3 (TA-10-2026-0091) restructures early intervention triggers for distressed banks, embedding proportionality for smaller institutions while hardening resolution criteria for systemically important ones. DGSD2 (TA-10-2026-0090) expands deposit guarantee coverage and cross-border cooperation protocols. SRMR3 remains the contested pillar — passed with ECR abstentions because it extends the Single Resolution Fund's backstop authority.

The Digital Omnibus AI (TA-10-2026-0098) represents Parliament's first significant simplification act for AI governance, reducing administrative burden for SMEs and startups classified under medium-risk categories. Its legislative significance is high because it demonstrates EP's willingness to course-correct on the AI Act's implementation burden within two years of the regulation entering force — a politically sensitive acknowledgement that initial compliance requirements were set too high.

Score sustained at 7/10: high legislative importance, but no new legislative development today. Confidence: 🟢 High (based on EP document data).

### Dimension 3: Coalition Sensitivity (7/10 ↑) 🟡
**Scope**: How fragile are the coalition alignments sustaining recent legislation?

ECR's SRMR3 abstention (documented in prior analysis) reveals the fault line: ECR's Polish and Italian wings object to any mechanism that could be used to mutualize banking losses across EU member states. If ECOFIN confirms full scope, the EPP must decide whether to hold the line or make concessions to ECR. Confidence: 🟡 Medium (coalition data low-confidence per EP API limitations).

### Dimension 4: Institutional Stakes (8/10) 🟢
**Scope**: What is the structural significance for EU institutional architecture?

Banking Union completion would mark the most significant single expansion of EU financial architecture since the Banking Union was established in 2014-2015. BRRD3+DGSD2+SRMR3 together create a genuinely integrated European financial safety net: a common resolution framework, harmonised deposit protection, and backstop funding — the three pillars originally envisioned at the height of the sovereign debt crisis. EP's role in adopting this package is constitutionally significant: under the ordinary legislative procedure, EP exercised co-decision authority to shape the final text, including inserting proportionality provisions over Commission objections.

The Digital Omnibus AI sets a precedent: EP can proactively simplify its own legislation within a parliamentary term, rather than waiting for Commission review. This institutional capacity for legislative correction is significant for EU governance. However, it requires Commission implementing acts to operationalise — if the Commission delays, Parliament has limited enforcement tools. Confidence: 🟢 High.
