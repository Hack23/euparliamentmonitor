# Implementation Feasibility — April 2026 EP Legislative Outputs
**Date:** 2026-05-16 | **Grade:** B2

## Feasibility Assessment Framework

Each adopted text is assessed on three implementation dimensions:
- **Legal Feasibility (LF):** Is the legal architecture in place for implementation?
- **Resource Feasibility (RF):** Does the responsible institution have capacity to implement?
- **Political Feasibility (PF):** Is there sufficient political will across institutions?

Score: 1 (infeasible) to 5 (immediately feasible)

## Item-by-Item Feasibility

### TA-10-2026-0160 — DMA Enforcement
- **LF: 5/5** — DMA legal basis fully established; enforcement powers in Article 26 operative
- **RF: 3/5** — DG COMP has enforcement powers but needs 3x current staffing for full capacity
- **PF: 4/5** — Cross-party parliamentary support; Commission has political incentive to act
- **Overall: 4/5 — FEASIBLE WITH CONSTRAINTS**
- **Key bottleneck:** DG COMP resource gap. Commission could resolve this with supplementary
  budget request in Q3 2026. Timeline for meaningful enforcement: 6-12 months.

### TA-10-2026-0161 — Ukraine Accountability
- **LF: 4/5** — Legal architecture for asset seizure mechanism under construction; some
  Treaty ambiguity on retroactive seizure
- **RF: 4/5** — EEAS has capacity; Ukraine Support Instrument administrative machinery operating
- **PF: 5/5** — Coalition Delta consensus; G7 backing
- **Overall: 4.3/5 — LARGELY FEASIBLE**
- **Key bottleneck:** Legal architecture for actual asset seizure (not just interest use)
  requires additional Council agreement. ECJ advisory opinion request on Treaty compatibility
  may be needed.

### TA-10-2026-0112 — Budget Guidelines 2027
- **LF: 5/5** — Standard annual procedure; legal basis TFEU Article 314
- **RF: 5/5** — Commission, Council, Parliament all have budget procedure machinery
- **PF: 3/5** — Parliament-Council trilogue will be contentious; defense vs climate trade-offs
- **Overall: 4.3/5 — PROCEDURALLY FEASIBLE; outcomes uncertain**
- **Key bottleneck:** Political negotiation on defense spending instrument. Treaty would
  need reform for NextGenerationEU II common borrowing; Council unlikely to agree in 2026.

### TA-10-2026-0162 — Armenia Resilience
- **LF: 4/5** — CEPA II signed; ratification procedure clear
- **RF: 3/5** — EEAS needs to mobilize member-state ratification coordination in 27 capitals
- **PF: 4/5** — Cross-bloc majority; Council generally supportive
- **Overall: 3.7/5 — FEASIBLE BUT SLOW**
- **Key bottleneck:** 27-member-state ratification (mixed agreement); one or two holdouts
  possible (Hungary, Slovakia given Armenia's anti-CSTO pivot).

### TA-10-2026-0157 — Livestock Sustainability
- **LF: 2/5** — No binding legal instrument exists; Commission needs to propose one
- **RF: 2/5** — No dedicated implementation machinery; DG AGRI reform needed
- **PF: 3/5** — "Balance" language helps politically but creates implementation ambiguity
- **Overall: 2.3/5 — LOW FEASIBILITY**
- **Key bottleneck:** Absence of binding legal instrument. Resolution cannot be "implemented"
  — it can only influence Commission's future proposal timeline.

### TA-10-2026-0163 — Online Exploitation
- **LF: 2/5** — Chat Control Regulation not yet proposed; underlying Directive structure contested
- **RF: 3/5** — DG HOME has capacity but implementation requires Big Tech cooperation
- **PF: 3/5** — Parliament split on CSS; Council similarly divided
- **Overall: 2.7/5 — MODERATE FEASIBILITY**
- **Key bottleneck:** Commission Chat Control proposal still pending; constitutional risk
  means implementation could be blocked by ECJ post-adoption.
