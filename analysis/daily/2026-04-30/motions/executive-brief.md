# Executive Brief — EP Motions, 23–30 April 2026

## Three Decisions Riding On This Week

1. **The immunity-waiver cluster sets a structural precedent for EP-domestic-judiciary coordination.** Three waivers in one session, all targeting right-flank MEPs facing home-country judicial proceedings, demonstrates that the JURI Committee is operationally aligned with Polish and Romanian prosecutorial authorities and willing to act in concert. This is consequential because it converts the EP's rhetorical rule-of-law doctrine into a *procedural reflex*. ECR's internal cohesion is the variable most sensitive to this precedent — the group must choose between defending colleagues facing plausible criminal proceedings (alienating its centrist Polish faction) or acquiescing (alienating its activist wing). *Confidence: HIGH on the pattern; MODERATE on ECR's strategic response.*

2. **The 2027 budget guidelines formally lock the EP into a defence-plus-climate fiscal posture.** TA-0112 is the EP's opening salvo in the 2027 cycle and signals continuity with the post-ReArm Europe debate: elevated defence-readiness spending, sustained Ukraine support, cohesion floor protection, and climate-transition envelopes preserved. The strategic question is whether the EPP-S&D-Renew working majority can defend this composite line against (a) ECR/PfE pressure to cut climate spending and (b) S&D/Greens pressure to expand social envelopes. *Confidence: HIGH.*

3. **The Şoşoacă waiver is the EP's clearest enforcement of behavioural norms inside the chamber.** Şoşoacă has been excluded from multiple group affiliations over the term and the waiver advances proceedings related to Romanian judicial processes. Operationally, the waiver is procedural; reputationally, it is a marker of how far the EP will go in supporting national authorities against an MEP whose behaviour has consistently breached chamber decorum. The precedent value is high for any future case involving NI or marginal-group MEPs. *Confidence: HIGH on the procedural fact; LOW on broader generalisation.*

Three immunity waivers, the budget cycle opener, a GHG-transport regulatory act, and a companion-animal welfare law — none of these are dramatic on their own, but read together they show the EP's mainstream coalition exercising the **full institutional toolkit**: budgetary authority, rule-of-law enforcement, sectoral regulation, and welfare policy. Importantly, the **EPP supports each of the four flagship votes**, including the immunity waivers — confirming that the EPP's institutionalist wing dominates when the file is procedural / rule-of-law rather than economic-regulatory.

The political-arithmetic context remains the same: 719 MEPs, 9 groups, EPP at 25.7 %, no durable supermajority. The EPP + S&D + Renew bloc (~397) is comfortably above 361 majority on rule-of-law and institutional files; it is *contested* on environmental and economic-regulatory files (the prior week's DMA-substance EPP cohesion at ~50 %). This week's flagship votes are procedural / institutional, which is precisely the territory where the bloc is most stable.

The **rule-of-law signal** sent by three right-flank waivers in one session has strategic resonance beyond Strasbourg. Polish and Romanian prosecutorial cooperation with the JURI Committee marks the maturation of EU-domestic judicial coordination architecture. Watch whether Hungarian authorities follow with a comparable request in 2026 H2 — that would be the structural test of whether this is a Polish-Romanian anomaly or a new EU norm.
