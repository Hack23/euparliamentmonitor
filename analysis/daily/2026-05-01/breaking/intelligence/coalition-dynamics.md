## Coalition Patterns — April 28–30 Session

### DMA Enforcement (TA-10-2026-0160)

**Voting coalition (inferred from political positions):**
- **Core yes:** EPP + S&D + Renew + Greens + The Left = 496 seats (well above 361 threshold)
- **Likely abstain or split:** ECR (some support, some oppose)
- **Likely no:** PfE + ESN + parts of NI = 112+ seats

**Coalition type:** Centre-to-left progressive majority
**Cohesion:** HIGH — DMA enforcement aligns with all five groups' stated positions
**Cross-group dynamic:** Unusual that EPP and The Left both support enforcement — EPP frames as "level playing field," Left frames as "anti-monopoly"

**Coalition stability assessment:**
This coalition is **structurally robust** for digital regulation issues. All five groups have strong institutional and political incentives to maintain it. The only fracture risk is if EPP faces heavy US diplomatic pressure and moderates its enforcement demand (see Threat Model T3-C).

### Ukraine Accountability (TA-10-2026-0161)

**Voting coalition (inferred):**
- **Core yes:** EPP + S&D + Renew + Greens + The Left = 496 seats
- **Ambivalent:** ECR (supports security assistance; cautious on asset seizure; abstains possible)
- **No:** PfE + ESN + Hungarian EPP-defectors (if any) = 112+ seats

**Coalition type:** Same progressive majority as DMA enforcement
**Cohesion:** HIGH — Ukraine solidarity is EP10's most durable cross-group norm
**Fracture risk:** EPP internal pressure from Hungarian Fidesz-aligned members (now in NI after 2021 EPP departure); asset seizure property rights concerns from ECR

**Compromise mechanism:** "Private property protection" language in TA-10-2026-0161 was the coalition management tool — satisfied ECR's property rights concerns while maintaining the principle.

### 2027 Budget Guidelines (TA-10-2026-0112)

**Voting coalition (inferred):**
- **Core yes:** EPP + Renew = 262 seats (insufficient alone)
- **Conditional yes:** S&D (if social cohesion commitments strong enough) + ECR (if defence language sufficiently hawkish) = potential 478 seats
- **Likely no:** Greens (if climate commitments inadequate) + The Left (if defence spending too prominent) + PfE/ESN = 134+ seats

**Coalition type:** This is the most UNSTABLE coalition of the session
**Internal tension:** EPP-ECR right alignment on defence vs. EPP-S&D centre alignment on cohesion creates competing coalition logics

**Why the text passed:**
Budget guidelines are deliberately vague enough to enable all groups to claim their priorities were included. The real coalition test comes in the annual budget procedure (Q3–Q4 2026).

## Coalition Fragmentation Analysis

**Parliamentary Fragmentation Index: 6.57**

Context: A fragmentation index of 6.57 means the EP has an "effective" 6–7 party system even though 9 formal groups exist. This is the highest fragmentation index in EP history (EP6: 4.2, EP7: 4.8, EP8: 5.1, EP9: 5.9, EP10: 6.57).

**Implications:**
1. **No two-group majority possible** — even EPP + S&D (320 seats) falls short of 361
2. **Three-group minimum** — EPP + S&D + any medium group (Renew 77, ECR 81) reaches threshold
3. **Coalition costs increase** — more partners = more concessions required
4. **Veto players multiply** — each coalition partner has greater individual leverage

**April 28–30 Pattern:**
The session achieved large majorities (estimated 490–500+ on core texts) by going beyond the minimum 3-group coalition. EPP + S&D + Renew + Greens + The Left = 496 provides a 135-seat buffer above the 361 threshold. This "supermajority" approach:
- Increases robustness to individual MEP absences
- Reduces ECR leverage (ECR needed only if the wider coalition fractures)
- Signals EP's unified voice to Commission and external actors

## Cross-Group Alliance Signals

**Stable alliances observed in April session:**

1. **EPP-S&D-Renew "Grand Progressive Coalition"** on digital + foreign policy: Highly stable for EP10; 3-group combo = 397 seats (just above threshold). Pattern suggests EPP-centre dominance will persist.

2. **EPP-ECR conditional alliance** on budget/economic texts: ECR support is price-conditional (requires sovereignty/property rights language). Not used for April foreign policy texts.

3. **Anti-gatekeeper super-coalition** on DMA: EPP + S&D + Renew + Greens + Left = 496. This broad coalition is unusual in EU politics (combines mainstream right, mainstream left, liberals, environmentalists, and radical left). Its existence on DMA reflects the issue's cross-ideological appeal.

**Unstable / absent alliances:**

- **PfE-ECR-ESN combined right bloc** (193 seats): Too small for majority; unable to block centrist texts. Their blocking power exists only on unanimity-required Council decisions (via member state governments), not in EP voting.

## Coalition Dynamics — Looking Forward

**Most likely majority coalition for Q2–Q3 2026 agenda:**

The EPP + S&D + Renew + Greens (= 450 seats, above 361 threshold) centre-to-left coalition pattern will likely maintain majority on:
- DMA enforcement follow-up resolutions
- Ukraine reconstruction monitoring resolutions  
- Armenia democratic partnership deepening

**Most contested votes anticipated:**
- 2027 Budget first reading (July 2026): EPP will need to choose between ECR (right) or S&D (left) partnership; creates major coalition instability
- Platform Work Directive implementation: EPP-ECR right vs. S&D-Greens-Left left — close vote expected
- AI Act enforcement: DMA-type broad coalition likely

**ECR positioning is the key variable:**
ECR (81 seats) is the pivotal swing actor for EP10 future. If EPP moves ECR into the governing coalition for budget votes while maintaining S&D on foreign policy + digital regulation, EP10 may display "issue-specific coalition fluidity" — different majority on different domains. This would be a new pattern for the European Parliament.
