# Stakeholder Map — EP Breaking: DMA Enforcement & Ukraine Accountability
**Date:** 2026-05-01 | **Article Type:** breaking | **Confidence:** 🟢 HIGH

---

## Stakeholder Mapping Framework

This analysis applies the ICAP (Interest, Capacity, Alignment, Power) framework to map stakeholders affected by the EP's April 28–30, 2026 decisions. Stakeholders are grouped across four tiers:
- **Tier 1 — Direct Institutional Actors** (EP political groups, Commission, Council)
- **Tier 2 — Regulated/Targeted Actors** (Tech platforms, member states, third countries)
- **Tier 3 — Civil Society/Media** (advocacy groups, journalists, civil society)
- **Tier 4 — Broader Public** (EU citizens, consumers, international community)

---

## Tier 1 — Direct Institutional Actors

### European Parliament — Political Groups

**EPP (European People's Party) — 185 seats | 25.73%**
- **Interest:** Regulatory rigour on digital markets (competitiveness framing); strong Ukraine solidarity (geopolitical values); fiscal responsibility on 2027 budget
- **Capacity:** HIGHEST — pivot group for all majorities; chairs key committees (IMCO, AFET, BUDG)
- **Alignment on DMA:** ✅ SUPPORT — EPP positioned DMA enforcement as "making EU companies' competitors play by the rules"
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ✅ SUPPORT — with compromise language on private property protection in asset seizure
- **Key concerns:** Avoid anti-competitiveness narrative; maintain US-EU tech relations; credible enforcement without over-reach
- **Forward posture:** EPP will push Commission to accelerate non-compliance proceedings; monitor Q3 2026 DMA enforcement decisions
- **Power rating:** 🔴 CRITICAL

**S&D (Socialists and Democrats) — 135 seats | 18.78%**
- **Interest:** Worker protection dimensions of DMA (platform labour); robust Ukraine solidarity; social justice in budget priorities
- **Capacity:** HIGH — second largest group; essential for centre-left majority
- **Alignment on DMA:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT — platform workers' rights as additional DMA dimension
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT — most hawkish on accountability and asset seizure
- **Key concerns:** Workers on gig platforms (not directly covered by DMA but linked); Ukraine war crimes accountability
- **Forward posture:** S&D will push for DMA enforcement milestones on a specific timetable; advocate for ICC strengthening
- **Power rating:** 🔴 HIGH

**Renew Europe — 77 seats | 10.71%**
- **Interest:** Pro-competition, pro-innovation; moderate Ukraine solidarity; liberal on data privacy
- **Capacity:** MEDIUM-HIGH — decisive swing bloc; LIBE committee representation
- **Alignment on DMA:** ✅ SUPPORT — with caveats on innovation-friendly enforcement approach
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ✅ SUPPORT — strongest on EU-Ukraine integration track
- **Key concerns:** Regulatory over-reach risk; avoid permanent competitive disadvantage for European tech startups
- **Forward posture:** Renew will seek Commission commitment to SME exemptions from DMA obligations; push EU-Ukraine accession track
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM-HIGH

**Greens/EFA — 53 seats | 7.37%**
- **Interest:** Digital rights, data privacy, platform accountability; climate mainstreaming in budget; strong Ukraine/Armenia democracy support
- **Capacity:** MEDIUM — often decisive for supermajorities; LIBE/ENVI/AFET presence
- **Alignment on DMA:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT — Greens advocated for even stronger enforcement; environmental data centre standards
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT — with climate-compatible reconstruction conditions
- **Key concerns:** DMA misses environmental standards for data centres; budget climate mainstreaming declining
- **Forward posture:** Greens will push for environmental addendum to DMA enforcement mandate; monitor climate% in 2027 budget
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM

**The Left — 46 seats | 6.4%**
- **Interest:** Anti-monopoly digital regulation; solidarity with Ukraine workers and civil society; anti-corporate dimension
- **Capacity:** MEDIUM — coalition partner for progressive majorities
- **Alignment on DMA:** ✅ SUPPORT — from anti-monopoly perspective; concerned about data commodification
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ✅ SUPPORT — with strong emphasis on war crimes documentation
- **Key concerns:** Tech platform worker exploitation not fully addressed; corporate tax avoidance linked to gatekeeper profitability
- **Forward posture:** The Left will raise DMA links to platform worker rights in EMPL committee
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM-LOW

**ECR (European Conservatives and Reformists) — 81 seats | 11.27%**
- **Interest:** Pro-business regulatory approach; conditional Ukraine support; national sovereignty on asset seizure
- **Capacity:** HIGH — third largest group; increasingly pivotal for EPP-led right majorities
- **Alignment on DMA:** ⚠️ SPLIT — some MEPs support level playing field argument; others see DMA as regulatory over-reach
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ⚠️ CONDITIONAL SUPPORT — strong on security assistance, cautious on asset seizure (private property concerns)
- **Key concerns:** Excessive regulatory burden on businesses; private property rights in asset confiscation; sovereignty concerns
- **Forward posture:** ECR will table amendments to weaken DMA enforcement milestones; push for private property carve-out in asset recovery regulation
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM-HIGH

**PfE (Patriots for Europe) — 85 seats | 11.82%**
- **Interest:** Anti-regulation; limited Ukraine support; national sovereignty
- **Capacity:** MEDIUM — oppositional; rarely in governing coalition on EP10 key votes
- **Alignment on DMA:** ❌ OPPOSE — frames as regulatory overreach harming free markets and US tech relationships
- **Alignment on Ukraine:** ❌ OPPOSE — opposes asset seizure; some members favour negotiated peace without accountability
- **Key concerns:** EU regulatory state expansion; Russian asset confiscation precedent for private property
- **Forward posture:** PfE will use DMA enforcement issue to build EU skepticism narrative; coordinate with Hungary's veto threat in Council on asset recovery
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (blocking minority on some issues)

**ESN (European Sovereign Nations) — 27 seats | 3.76%**
- **Interest:** Anti-regulation; pro-Russia elements; anti-Ukraine aid
- **Capacity:** LOW — small group; largely oppositional
- **Alignment on both texts:** ❌ OPPOSE
- **Power rating:** 🟢 LOW

### European Commission

**Directorate-General for Competition (DG COMP)**
- **Interest:** EP enforcement demand creates political accountability for DMA proceedings
- **Capacity:** HIGHEST on DMA — sole enforcement authority; holds investigation powers
- **Response to EP resolution:** Must now provide formal EP response within 3 months per Rule 233
- **Forward posture:** DG COMP Executive VP (Margrethe Vestager's successor) will face increased scrutiny on enforcement timetables; likely to accelerate at least one high-profile DMA enforcement decision before Q4 2026 to demonstrate responsiveness
- **Power rating:** 🔴 CRITICAL on DMA enforcement

**EEAS (European External Action Service)**
- **Interest:** Ukraine resolution + Armenia text feed directly into EEAS diplomatic strategy
- **Capacity:** HIGH on foreign policy coordination
- **Forward posture:** EEAS will incorporate EP positions into forthcoming diplomatic communications with Kyiv, Yerevan, and Baku
- **Power rating:** 🔴 HIGH

**DG BUDG**
- **Interest:** 2027 budget guidelines must be incorporated into Commission budget proposal
- **Capacity:** HIGH — controls Commission budget draft
- **Forward posture:** Commission budget proposal (May 2026) must respond to EP priority lines (defence, clean transition)
- **Power rating:** 🔴 HIGH

## Tier 2 — Regulated/Targeted Actors

### Major Digital Platform Operators (DMA Gatekeepers)

**Apple Inc.**
- **Interest:** Minimise DMA compliance costs; challenge enforcement interpretations
- **Capacity:** Extremely high legal/lobbying resources
- **DMA exposure:** Highest — App Store, iOS interoperability, browser choice screen
- **Expected response to EP enforcement demand:** Accelerate parallel CJEU challenge to gatekeeper designation; intensify Commission negotiations; engage MEPs on innovation impact
- **Economic exposure:** Potential fines up to 10% of global annual turnover (≈$40bn on $400bn revenue base)
- **Power rating:** 🔴 HIGH (defensive)

**Alphabet/Google**
- **Interest:** Maintain current market positions; challenge DMA obligation scope
- **DMA exposure:** High — Google Shopping, Google Play, Google Search self-preferencing
- **Expected response:** Compliance theatre on some obligations; legal challenge on others; lobbying through US business associations (AmCham EU)
- **Power rating:** 🔴 HIGH (defensive)

**Meta Platforms**
- **Interest:** Interoperability obligations on Messenger/WhatsApp most threatening
- **DMA exposure:** Medium-high — messaging interoperability, advertising data separation
- **Expected response:** Implement minimum viable interoperability; lobby EP LIBE on privacy precedent of messaging interoperability
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM-HIGH (defensive)

**Amazon**
- **Interest:** Marketplace self-preferencing prohibition most critical
- **DMA exposure:** Medium — retail marketplace, AWS/data portability
- **Expected response:** Structural changes to marketplace ranking algorithm; legal challenge on data portability scope
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (defensive)

### Member States — Ukraine Asset Seizure

**Belgium (Euroclear)**
- **Interest:** Euroclear holds ~€180bn of frozen Russian sovereign assets; already channelling windfall profits to Ukraine
- **Capacity:** HIGH — hosts the key mechanism
- **Position:** Broadly supportive of EP resolution; cautious on full confiscation of principal (legal liability concerns)
- **Forward posture:** Will seek clarification of member state liability under international law before supporting principal confiscation

**Germany**
- **Interest:** Large bilateral support commitments to Ukraine; cautious on asset confiscation precedent (sovereign immunity concerns)
- **Position:** Supports windfall profits transfer; cautious on principal confiscation
- **Forward posture:** Likely to seek robust legal opinion from CJEU before supporting principal transfer

**Hungary**
- **Interest:** Blocks EU Ukraine decisions in Council; aligned with Russian interests
- **Position:** OPPOSE — has used veto on multiple Ukraine support measures
- **Forward posture:** Will attempt to block Council adoption of asset recovery regulation
- **Risk:** Hungary's veto creates structural gap between EP aspirations and Council reality

### Armenia and Azerbaijan

**Republic of Armenia**
- **Interest:** EP resolution provides international legitimacy for democratic consolidation
- **Capacity:** LIMITED in EU institutional context
- **Forward posture:** Armenian government expected to cite EP resolution in EU partnership negotiations; use as leverage with domestic audience
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (receiver of signal)

**Republic of Azerbaijan**
- **Interest:** EP resolution on Armenia could be read as indirect criticism; monitoring for Nagorno-Karabakh context
- **Position:** Will watch how EU-Armenia partnership develops; maintaining good relations with EU on energy supply (post-Russia gas alternatives)
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (external actor)

## Tier 3 — Civil Society and Media

**Digital Rights NGOs (EDRi, Access Now)**
- **Interest:** DMA enforcement as template for digital rights protection
- **Alignment:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT for EP enforcement demand
- **Forward posture:** Will mobilise public campaigns pressure-testing Commission enforcement timeline
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (agenda-setting)

**Platform Workers' Organisations**
- **Interest:** DMA obligations linked to gig economy reform
- **Alignment:** Partial — DMA does not directly address labour rights but creates accountability precedent
- **Forward posture:** Push EP EMPL committee to table platform work resolution using DMA enforcement precedent

**Ukrainian Civil Society**
- **Interest:** Accountability for Russian war crimes; reconstruction funding access
- **Alignment:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT
- **Forward posture:** Will continue lobbying EU institutions on ICPA strengthening and asset seizure acceleration

**Armenian Diaspora NGOs (France, Belgium)**
- **Interest:** EU strategic partnership for Armenia; democratic support
- **Alignment:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (lobbying capacity)

**Tech Industry Lobby Groups (DigitalEurope, Computer & Communications Industry Association)**
- **Interest:** Moderate DMA enforcement interpretation; protect innovation ecosystem
- **Alignment:** ⚠️ PARTIAL — support "proportionate" enforcement; oppose structural remedies
- **Forward posture:** Will engage Commission DG COMP to shape enforcement methodologies

## Tier 4 — Broader Public

**EU Consumers (digital markets)**
- **Interest:** Better interoperability, data portability, and platform competition
- **Alignment:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT (72% in surveys support DMA objectives)
- **Power rating:** 🟢 LOW (indirect; expressed through EP elections)

**EU Citizens — Ukraine**
- **Interest:** Accountable resolution of Ukraine war; controlled costs of EU support
- **Alignment:** 🟡 MIXED — majority support accountability; growing minority experiences support fatigue
- **Power rating:** 🟢 LOW (expressed through national elections feeding EP composition)

**Companion Animal Owners (dogs/cats regulation)**
- **Interest:** Traceability and welfare protection for pets
- **Alignment:** ✅ STRONG SUPPORT
- **Numbers affected:** ~90 million EU pet owners
- **Power rating:** 🟡 MEDIUM (strong civic mobilisation potential on animal welfare)
