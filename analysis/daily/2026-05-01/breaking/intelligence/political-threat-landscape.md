## Threat Cluster 1: European Parliament Internal Threats

**[WEP-HIGH] Centre Coalition Erosion on Budget (60–70%)**
The April 28 budget guidelines adopted via "something for everyone" language. When Q3 2026 budget first reading forces actual numerical trade-offs between defence, climate, and cohesion, the centre coalition will fracture. EPP will face a binary choice: right-ward coalition with ECR (defence priority) or leftward coalition with S&D/Greens (climate priority). Either choice fragments the current supermajority.

*EP institutional impact:* Budget vote majority narrows significantly; PfE/ECR gain agenda influence; climate mainstreaming percentage at risk.

**[WEP-MEDIUM] EP Enforcement Demand Perceived as Symbolic (50%)**
If the Commission does not produce a concrete DMA enforcement action within 6 months of the April 30 resolution, political momentum behind EP enforcement demands weakens. PfE/ECR narrative — "EP passes resolutions that nobody implements" — gains traction with EP10's new MEPs and their constituents.

*EP institutional impact:* Future enforcement demands lose credibility; institutional role as accountability body undermined.

**[WEP-LOW] Major EP Internal Scandal (5%)**
While Qatargate (2022) probability of recurrence within 4 years is very low, any new major corruption scandal would dominate EP agenda, potentially suspending normal legislative business for weeks.

## Threat Cluster 2: EU Institutional Threats

**[WEP-HIGH] Commission Slow Response to Rule 233 Obligations (75%)**
Commission has historically responded to EP parliamentary questions and resolution-triggered inquiries with generic language that satisfies procedural requirements while committing to nothing specific. This pattern is almost certain to recur.

*EP institutional impact:* Accountability gap between EP aspiration and Commission action; creates cynicism about EP's oversight role.

**[WEP-MEDIUM] Council-EP Institutional Rift on Ukraine (40%)**
If Hungary's Council veto persists and Council adopts softer Ukraine asset recovery language than EP's resolution demands, an overt Council-EP institutional rift emerges. This would be framed publicly as "Council sacrificing accountability for diplomatic flexibility."

*EP institutional impact:* Interinstitutional relations strained; EP AFET committee escalates criticism of Council; potential extraordinary plenary session.

**[WEP-LOW] Commission Dismissed via EP Censure (3%)**
In extreme scenarios, if Commission fails to implement major legislative mandates (DMA + Ukraine asset recovery), EP could initiate a censure vote (Article 234 TFEU). Requires absolute majority (360+ MEPs). Unlikely but precedent exists (1999).

## Threat Cluster 3: Member State Threats

**[WEP-HIGH] Hungarian Veto Persists (70%)**
Based on Hungary's track record (2022–2025), continued blocking of Ukraine-related Council decisions is the baseline expectation. The April 30 EP resolution has no mechanism to override Hungarian sovereignty.

*Political threat:* Structural gap between EP aspirations and Council implementation; damages EP's credibility.

*Mitigation available:* Enhanced cooperation (Art. 20 TEU) with 26 member states — EP should formally support this.

**[WEP-MEDIUM] German Political Uncertainty Modulates EU Leadership (40%)**
Germany's current coalition faces internal tensions over defence spending, Ukraine aid levels, and economic policy. Shifts in German government position could moderate EU's unified response to either Ukraine or DMA enforcement.

*Political threat:* Germany as swing state in Council — if German government moderates, Council majority on Ukraine texts could narrow.

**[WEP-LOW] French-German Axis Breakdown (20%)**
While French-German bilateral leadership has been tested in EP10 (different priorities on defence vs. climate), a genuine Franco-German breakdown on a specific EU file remains unlikely in the 6-month horizon.

## Threat Cluster 4: Foreign State Threats

**[WEP-HIGH] Russian Information Operations Targeting EP (85%)**
Documented Russian state-linked information operations targeting EU institutions are ongoing and will certainly intensify following the Ukraine accountability resolution. Specific threat vectors:
- Social media amplification of PfE/ESN minority voices as "EU alternative"
- Selective document leaks designed to embarrass EP AFET committee
- Targeting of specific MEPs in key committees (AFET, LIBE, BUDG)
- Coordinated narrative: "EP accountability resolution prolongs war; peace requires pragmatism"

*Political threat:* Gradual erosion of Ukraine solidarity in EU member state domestic politics; creates political space for PfE/ECR accommodation of Russian narratives.

**[WEP-MEDIUM] US Tech Lobby Coordinated Campaign Against EP (45%)**
Following the DMA enforcement resolution, US tech companies and the US government are likely to coordinate a public-facing campaign:
- "DMA discriminates against US companies"
- "EP enforcement demand threatens EU-US digital trade"
- Commission under diplomatic pressure to demonstrate "proportionate" enforcement

*Political threat:* Creates political cost for EPP's enforcement stance; potential EPP fragmentation if US pressure becomes an EPP internal debate topic.

**[WEP-MEDIUM] China Exploits DMA Enforcement Asymmetry (35%)**
If DMA enforcement is perceived as selectively targeting US platforms while treating Chinese platforms (ByteDance/TikTok) more leniently for diplomatic reasons, China may publicly amplify this inconsistency to undermine EU rule of law credibility.

*Political threat:* EP's DMA enforcement demand loses moral authority if seen as geopolitically selective rather than legally neutral.

## Threat Cluster 5: Electoral/Democratic Threats

**[WEP-LOW] EP 2024 Mandate Credibility Challenge (15%)**
If far-right groups (PfE + ESN = 112 seats) argue that the April 28–30 EP session exceeded its mandate — specifically on Ukraine accountability asset seizure — they could create a public narrative challenging the legitimacy of the majority coalition.

*Political threat:* "EU institution acting beyond its mandate" narrative resonates in some member states; creates domestic political cost for MEPs in Hungary, Italy, France (RN-aligned).

**Mitigation:** Legal analysis demonstrating EP acts within treaty competences; public communication emphasising EP mandate from June 2024 elections.
