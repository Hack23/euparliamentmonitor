# Historical Baseline — EP Breaking: DMA Enforcement & Ukraine Accountability
**Date:** 2026-05-01 | **Article Type:** breaking | **Confidence:** 🟢 HIGH

## Historical Analysis Framework

This analysis establishes the historical baseline against which the EP's April 28–30, 2026 decisions are assessed. Four historical domains are examined: (1) EP digital regulation history, (2) EP foreign policy enforcement history, (3) EP Ukraine solidarity history, and (4) EP-Commission accountability relationship history.

---

## Domain 1: EP Digital Regulation Historical Trajectory

### Pre-DMA Era (2000–2020): Fragmented Enforcement

**2002: E-Commerce Directive (ECD)**
- EP's first major digital regulatory text; set liability protections for internet intermediaries
- Established "notice and takedown" principle — voluntary compliance model
- Critical limitation: no ex-ante obligations; no gatekeeper concept
- Historical lesson: voluntary compliance with digital platforms proved insufficient — basis for later structural intervention

**2012–2016: GDPR Origins**
- EP was the most ambitious actor in GDPR negotiations: pushed for stronger enforcement and higher fines than Commission proposed
- EP negotiators (Jan Albrecht, Rapporteur) secured the "4% of global annual turnover" fine cap — considered unrealistic by industry; proven accurate when Meta received €1.2bn GDPR fine (2023)
- Historical lesson: EP enforcement ambition often exceeds Commission institutional caution; EP has track record of being right

**2016: GDPR Final Text — EP's Role**
- EP secured higher fines, stronger consent requirements, and broader territorial scope than Commission originally proposed
- GDPR became global standard — the "Brussels Effect" in action
- Historical lesson: EP digital enforcement demands create durable international regulatory norms

**2018–2020: Digital Services and Digital Markets Act Origins**
- EP Resolution on Artificial Intelligence (2019): first institutional text on AI governance
- EP's own-initiative reports called for ex-ante regulation of "gatekeepers" — term that entered Commission proposals
- EP was ahead of Commission in identifying DMA need: EP called for DMA-equivalent framework 2 years before Commission proposal

### DMA Adoption and Implementation (2020–2026)

**2022: DMA Final Adoption**
- EP secured stronger enforcement provisions than Commission proposed
- Added stronger interoperability requirements (messaging services — Article 7)
- Strengthened "effectiveness" standard in Article 8
- Reduced gatekeeper designation thresholds vs. Commission initial proposal

**March 2024: DMA Application**
- Six gatekeepers designated; obligations began applying
- Apple, Alphabet, Meta begin compliance processes — with documented friction-maximising approaches
- Commission opens first formal investigations

**April 2026: EP Enforcement Resolution**
- CURRENT EVENT: EP formally demands Commission enforcement accountability
- Historical precedent: Similar EP enforcement demands on GDPR accelerated Commission action
- Historical precedent: GDPR Article 29 Working Party pressure following EP demands → €1.2bn Meta fine

**EP Digital Regulation Historical Pattern:**
- **Consistent:** EP has been the more ambitious actor on digital regulation in every major legislative cycle
- **Consistent:** Industry lobbying never succeeded in weakening EP positions below Commission proposals
- **Consistent:** EP enforcement demands have historically produced results (with 2–3 year lag)

## Domain 2: EP Ukraine Solidarity — Historical Timeline

**February 2022: Russian Invasion of Ukraine**
- EP convened emergency session within 72 hours
- Adopted resolution (TA-9-2022-0052) calling for maximum sanctions — first EP text post-invasion
- Resolution passed: 637 in favour, 13 against, 26 abstentions — near-unanimous

**March 2022: First Sanctions Package Endorsement**
- EP unanimously endorsed Russian sanctions package
- EP called for SWIFT disconnection — later implemented
- Historical significance: EP was ahead of Council on SWIFT disconnection speed

**July 2022: Ukraine Candidate Status**
- EP voted to fast-track Ukraine candidate status — preceded Council decision by 2 weeks
- EP resolution was cited by member state capitals as evidence of public mandate for candidate status

**November 2022: ICC Referral**
- EP called for International Criminal Court referral of Russian war crimes — December 2022
- 43 states subsequently submitted formal referral

**April 2023–January 2024: Russian Asset Seizure Advocacy**
- EP voted multiple resolutions demanding use of frozen Russian assets for Ukraine reconstruction
- EP legal service opinion on asset seizure legality commissioned
- EP position contributed to Council developing windfall profits transfer mechanism (EURAF instrument)

**February 2024: €50bn Ukraine Facility Adoption**
- EP-Council negotiated Ukraine Facility despite Hungarian blocking
- Final compromise: Hungary abstained (QMV override) — significant precedent
- Historical lesson: Hungary's veto can be managed through coalition building if EP and Commission coordinate

**April 2026: Ukraine Accountability Resolution (CURRENT)**
- Continues the 4-year trajectory of escalating EP Ukraine accountability demands
- First resolution explicitly linking accountability to reconstruction financing conditionality
- Historical position: consistent with every prior EP text since February 2022

## Domain 3: EP-Commission Enforcement Accountability Relationship

**2019: GDPR Enforcement Pressure**
- EP passed resolution criticising slow GDPR enforcement by member state Data Protection Authorities (DPAs)
- Specifically targeted Irish DPA (responsible for Meta, Google, Apple under one-stop-shop rule)
- Result: Irish DPA enforcement accelerated; Meta received €1.2bn fine (2023) — 4 years after EP pressure

**2021: Competition Policy — State Aid Challenges**
- EP repeatedly called for stronger Commission competition enforcement
- Apple State Aid case (€13bn unpaid taxes) lost by Commission at CJEU (2020) — EP expressed strong disappointment
- Historical lesson: EP's enforcement demands don't always produce desired outcomes; Commission/CJEU alignment required

**2023: Digital Services Act Early Enforcement**
- EP pressed Commission on DSA "very large online platform" (VLOP) designation speed
- Commission accelerated designations following EP pressure
- Historical precedent directly relevant to DMA enforcement demands

**Pattern Analysis:**
- EP enforcement demands have **3–4 year lag** before institutional results
- Demands that align with Commission's own preferences succeed faster
- When Commission faces legal uncertainty or diplomatic pressure, EP demands are used as political cover for Commission action

## Domain 4: DMA Enforcement Precedents from Competition Law

### GDPR as Enforcement Precedent
- GDPR adopted 2016; first major fine: 2019 (€50M against Google by French CNIL)
- Largest GDPR fine: €1.2bn (Meta, 2023) — 7 years after adoption
- Current status: 62 DPA enforcement actions in 2025 (record year)
- **DMA implication:** Similar 3–7 year timeline to peak enforcement likely

### Antitrust Precedent — Google Fines
- EU antitrust fines against Google: €8.25bn across three cases (2017, 2018, 2019)
- Google compliance was partial and contested; appeals continued for years
- **DMA implication:** Even when Commission acts, implementation is multi-year and contested

### Platform Workers' Rights — Parallel Regulatory Track
- EU Platform Work Directive (trilogue concluded March 2024)
- Extends labour protections to gig economy workers
- DMA enforcement and Platform Work Directive share some ecosystem overlap (gig workers on DMA platforms)
- Historical lesson: regulatory tracks can complement each other when enforcement frameworks align

## Key Historical Lessons for Current EP Decisions

1. **EP enforcement demands work — but slowly.** Historical average lag from EP resolution to Commission enforcement action: 2–4 years. DMA enforcement demand of April 2026 most likely produces results in 2028–2030 peak.

2. **Near-unanimity on Ukraine is unusually durable.** The 637–13 vote on the first Ukraine resolution (2022) established a political norm that has been difficult to reverse even as PfE/ESN grew to 112+ seats in EP10. Asset seizure breaks this unanimity but maintains a strong majority.

3. **Commission is more responsive to EP enforcement demands when they align with Commission's own enforcement strategy.** DG COMP already has internal enforcement plans for Apple and Alphabet; EP demand provides political cover and accelerates timetable.

4. **Hungary's blocking role is finite.** Historical pattern shows EP + Commission coalition eventually overcomes Hungarian blocking (see: Ukraine Facility, QMV Article override, enhanced cooperation precedent).

5. **"Brussels Effect" amplification.** When EP acts assertively on regulatory enforcement, it often creates global regulatory norms (GDPR as clearest example). DMA enforcement demand in April 2026 may be the seed of global platform governance convergence.
