## EU Macroeconomic Context (Qualitative, Non-IMF)

**GDP Growth:**
- EU aggregate GDP growth in 2025 was estimated at ~0.9–1.2% (ECB staff macroeconomic projections, December 2025 cited in EP budget debate)
- 2026 growth projections: ~1.1–1.5% (ECB March 2026 projections referenced in ECON committee debate)
- Persistent competitiveness gap with US (2.8–3.0%) and China (4.5–5%) — Draghi Report (2024) core finding
- Germany: technical recession in Q3–Q4 2025 based on Bundesbank preliminary estimates cited in EP debate; recovery expected in H2 2026

**Inflation:**
- ECB target rate (2.0%) expected to be achieved in H2 2026 following tightening cycle
- Energy inflation: declining from post-Ukraine war peak; normalising in EU market
- Food inflation: moderating but above pre-2022 baseline

**Labour Market:**
- EU unemployment rate: ~6.0–6.2% (Eurostat Q1 2026, referenced in S&D budget debate contributions)
- Youth unemployment in southern member states: 15–20% range — persistent structural challenge

**External Trade:**
- EU trade surplus with some partners weakening as export competitiveness declines
- Trade relations with China: managed friction; bilateral investment agreement stalled
- Trade relations with US: significant risk from new tariff threats (see Threat Model, T3-C)
- EU current account: gradually improving following energy import normalisation

### Gatekeeper Market Economics (Estimates — not IMF)

**Combined EU revenue of DMA-designated gatekeepers (estimated 2025):**
- Alphabet (Google): EU revenues estimated €35–40bn from EU market operations
- Apple: EU revenues estimated €55–65bn (hardware + services combined)
- Meta: EU revenues estimated €18–22bn (digital advertising)
- Amazon: EU revenues estimated €35–45bn (marketplace + AWS)
- ByteDance (TikTok): EU revenues estimated €4–6bn (advertising)
- Microsoft: EU revenues estimated €25–30bn (enterprise software + cloud)
- **Total estimated gatekeeper EU revenues: ~€170–200bn annually**

**DMA compliance cost estimates:**
- European Commission Impact Assessment (2020, prior to adoption): €3–8bn cumulative compliance costs for designated gatekeepers over 5 years
- Gatekeeper estimates from industry lobbying: €15–25bn (inflated — includes strategic investments as compliance costs)
- Independent estimates (economists cited in IMCO committee reports): €5–10bn cumulative

**Consumer surplus potential from DMA compliance:**
- EC estimate: €10–15bn annually if full interoperability and data portability achieved
- Competition economics perspective: reduced switching costs → increased competitive pressure → price/quality improvements for consumers

**EU Digital SME impact:**
- Improved gatekeeper platform interoperability could enable 6.7M EU SMEs to expand market access
- Estimated EU SME revenue uplift from DMA compliance: €40–60bn annually (Commission DG GROW estimate referenced in IMCO committee)

**DMA fine risk for gatekeepers:**
- Maximum fine: 10% of global annual turnover for first violation
- Apple annual revenue (2025): ~$400bn → maximum fine €40bn (historically not reached; realistic fine range €3–10bn based on GDPR precedent scaling)
- Repeated violation: 20% global turnover — significant deterrent

## Ukraine Economic Dimension

**Frozen Russian Sovereign Assets (estimates):**
- Total frozen in EU: approximately €300bn (mainly in Belgium/Euroclear; with smaller amounts in Luxembourg and other financial centres)
- Windfall profits (interest on frozen assets): estimated €3–4bn annually at current rates
- Already transferred to Ukraine: €6.1bn windfall profits (February 2025 disbursement via EU4UkraineFund)

**Ukrainian Reconstruction Costs:**
- World Bank preliminary estimate: $500bn+ (2024 Ukraine Rapid Damage Assessment)
- Kyiv School of Economics: $486bn as of end-2024 (documented damage + reconstruction costs)
- EU contribution committed: €50bn (Ukraine Facility 2024–2027) + bilateral member state commitments

**EP Resolution Economic Implications:**
- Endorses use of principal (€300bn) for reconstruction — legally contested, economically transformative
- If principal confiscated and transferred: Ukraine reconstruction significantly accelerated
- EU budget impact: reduced need for direct EU budget transfers to Ukraine

**Macroeconomic Risk — Asset Seizure:**
- Precedent concern: international investors' perception of EU as jurisdiction for sovereign asset confiscation
- Russian government retaliation scenarios: seizure of EU-connected assets in Russia (estimated EU investment exposure in Russia: €80–100bn, mostly already written down)
- Financial market reaction: largely already priced in given assets have been frozen since 2022

## Budget 2027 Economic Framework

**EU Budget 2027 Priorities (from adopted EP guidelines, TA-10-2026-0112):**

1. **Defence (EDIP):** EP calls for increased EU budget support for European Defence Industrial Plan
   - Current EU defence-specific budget: approximately €8bn annually
   - Member state ambition: reach 2.5%+ of GDP by 2026–2027 (NATO+ commitment)
   - EU budget contribution to defence: expected to rise to €15–20bn by 2027 MFF revision

2. **Clean Industrial Deal:**
   - EU annual investment gap for clean transition: €260bn (Commission Sustainable Finance Advisory)
   - EU budget contribution expected: €70bn+ through 2027 (combination of existing and new instruments)
   - Risk: Zero-sum with defence spending given total MFF ceiling

3. **Agricultural Resilience:**
   - CAP budget baseline: €58.5bn (2024)
   - Climate extreme events increasing: EP calls for increased agricultural crisis reserve
   - Risk: Competition with other priorities under flat MFF

4. **Social Cohesion:**
   - European Social Fund+ (ESF+): €99.3bn (2021–2027 MFF)
   - EP calls for maintaining cohesion policy allocation percentages despite defence spending demands
   - Fundamental tension: member states and EP priorities directly conflict on cohesion vs. defence trade-off

**EU Budget Constraint:**
- MFF ceiling: 1.10% of EU GNI (EU gross national income)
- GNI-based constraint limits total flexibility; additional spending requires Treaty change or special instruments
- EU borrowing (NGEU model): potential for additional €100bn+ special borrowing instrument for defence/Ukraine
