## Event Classifications

### TIER II — MAJOR

**TA-10-2026-0160: Enforcement of the Digital Markets Act** *(adopted 2026-04-30)*
- **Primary Classification:** Digital Regulation / Competition Law Enforcement
- **Sub-classification:** Regulatory accountability escalation
- **Domain intersection:** DIGIT/ITRE/IMCO/LIBE
- **Geopolitical dimension:** EU-US tech relations; third-country gatekeeper operators
- **Reasoning:** The DMA is the EU's flagship digital competition regulation. The April 30 enforcement text represents the EP's transition from monitoring to demanding enforcement accountability — the first such structured demand in the DMA's enforcement lifecycle. This has direct legal consequences for Commission enforcement timetables and may trigger Article 26 (transparency reporting) obligations.
- **Precedent level:** HIGH — EP enforcement demands have historically accelerated Commission action (cf. GDPR enforcement pressure 2019–2021)

**TA-10-2026-0161: Ensuring accountability for Russia's attacks on Ukraine** *(adopted 2026-04-30)*
- **Primary Classification:** Common Foreign and Security Policy / International Accountability
- **Sub-classification:** Armed conflict accountability; asset seizure framework
- **Domain intersection:** AFET/BUDG/JURI
- **Geopolitical dimension:** Russia-Ukraine war; G7 coordination; ICC jurisdiction
- **Reasoning:** Adopted as EU-level trilogue discussions on the Asset Recovery and Restitution Regulation intensify. The EP text provides formal political backing for using frozen Russian sovereign assets for Ukrainian reconstruction — a position endorsed by EPP, S&D, Renew, and Greens but partially contested by ECR (reservations on private asset protection) and firmly opposed by PfE and ESN.
- **Coalition significance:** Cross-group majority (EPP+S&D+Renew+Greens+The Left = ~496 seats) secured by compromise language on private property protection.
