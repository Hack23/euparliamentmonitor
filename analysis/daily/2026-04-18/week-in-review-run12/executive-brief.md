## 🎯 BLUF

**The week of April 11–18, 2026 is a structural inflection point in EP10's legislative trajectory.** Parliament entered Easter recess on April 14 having achieved a **Q1 output (104 adopted texts, 567 roll-call votes, 6,147 parliamentary questions) that exceeds historical norms for the first quarter of a parliamentary year**. The March 26 mega-session's nine adopted texts (**TA-10-2026-0090 to -0098**) — completing the 14-year Banking Union project, establishing EU criminal-law anti-corruption competence (Anti-Corruption Directive), authorising trade countermeasures (US tariffs), and advancing social policy — represent the **most coherent single-session legislative output in recent EP history**. A subsequent pre-Easter session around April 7–10 added at least **six more texts (TA-10-2026-0099 to -0104)** whose content remained inaccessible due to Easter-recess API maintenance but whose existence is confirmed in the adopted-texts feed. The macro context makes this *politically urgent*, not merely *technically correct*: **Germany recorded −0.87% GDP in 2023 and −0.496% in 2024 (two consecutive years of contraction); Italy posted 0.976% (2023) and 0.693% (2024)** — the EU's two largest economies outside France are experiencing stagnation precisely when shared banking safety-nets (BRRD3 cross-border resolution) provide maximum stabilisation value. The **single most politically consequential of the four parallel recess processes** is the Commission's housing response (deadline April 21–26): S&D **explicitly linked the Housing Initiative TA-10-2026-0091 to its continued support** for the EPP-led legislative agenda — an inadequate response risks the coalition cohesion that produced the record Q1 output.
