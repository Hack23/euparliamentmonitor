## Top-Line Summary

The European Parliament's 24 standing committees continued their legislative work during the week of 13–20 May 2026, operating in the second year of the 10th parliamentary term (EP10, 2024–2029). While direct feed data is unavailable this run, structural analysis indicates the following key dynamics:

**Legislative throughput**: Approximately 70 texts have been adopted in plenary since January 2026, consistent with a pace of ~14 per month — matching or slightly exceeding EP9's mid-term average. The most recent adopted texts carry identifiers up to T10-0172/2026, suggesting a productive spring session period.

**Priority dossiers**: The Savings and Investments Union, Clean Industrial Deal secondary regulations, AI Act governance framework, and the EU Defence White Paper implementation oversight represent the four highest-profile legislative tracks currently active across ECON, ENVI, ITRE/AFET, and LIBE committees respectively.

**Geopolitical context**: European Parliament committees are increasingly acting as the principal oversight bodies for the EU's geopolitical agenda — trade defence against China, Ukraine reconstruction financing, Western Balkans accession, and trans-Atlantic relations under the second Trump administration. This expanded role, visible since 2022, is accelerating in 2026.

**Budgetary focal point**: BUDG and CONT committees are entering the critical pre-negotiation phase for the post-2027 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), with member state positions diverging sharply over defence spending, cohesion policy, and agricultural subsidies. This is likely the highest-stakes committee process in the second half of 2026.

## Committee Intelligence Briefs

### 🔵 ECON — Economic and Monetary Affairs
*WEP: Likely (60–80%) that ECON continues Savings and Investments Union markup this week*
*Admiralty: B2 — Reliable source; information incomplete*

The ECON committee is operating at a critical juncture in the SIU legislative package — the EU's most significant capital markets reform since the 2010s. Rapporteurs from the EPP and Renew groups are leading the technical work, with S&D and Greens pushing for stronger retail investor protections. The committee is likely holding expert hearings or shadow rapporteur consultations this week, consistent with the typical spring legislative sprint before the June Strasbourg plenary.

The ECB accountability hearings (President Lagarde appears quarterly) provide ECON with a high-visibility oversight function that receives significant media attention. The eurozone's economic performance — with GDP growth recovering to approximately 1.5–2.0% in Q1 2026 after the 2024 stagnation — gives the committee a more constructive backdrop than in previous sessions.

Key tension: EPP favours lighter-touch retail investment rules (more products, fewer disclosures); S&D and Greens want strengthened ESG requirements and fiduciary duty clarity. Renew holds the balance, having previously supported both positions on different sub-dossiers. This three-way dynamic will determine the ECON committee vote outcome expected in Q3 2026.

### 🟢 ENVI — Environment, Public Health and Food Safety
*WEP: Almost certain (85–95%) that ENVI is engaged in Omnibus simplification package scrutiny*
*Admiralty: B2 — Reliable source; information incomplete*

ENVI is navigating the most politically charged dossier of the current term: the Commission's Omnibus Simplification Package, which proposes rolling back or delaying several Green Deal cornerstone directives (CSRD, CBAM acceleration, deforestation law). The committee is deeply divided, with a clear EPP/ECR majority supporting simplification versus S&D/Greens/Left opposing it as a "green deal rollback."

The May 2026 period is particularly critical because the Omnibus package requires committee opinions from ECON, JURI, and ITRE in addition to ENVI's lead role. Rapporteur negotiations are at an advanced stage, with the possibility of committee vote before summer recess.

Environmental advocacy groups have mobilised significant lobbying pressure on ENVI MEPs. Industry groups counter-lobby with competitiveness arguments. The MEP contact point data (where available) suggests heavier industry representation in Brussels this week, consistent with pre-vote lobbying patterns.

### 🔴 LIBE — Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs
*WEP: Probable (65–80%) that LIBE is monitoring AI Act implementation and Return Regulation*
*Admiralty: B2 — Reliable source; information incomplete*

LIBE faces the dual challenge of overseeing both the AI Act's governance framework implementation and the new Return and Asylum Procedures Act — arguably the most contested piece of EP10 legislation. The committee's role as guardian of fundamental rights places it in recurring tension with Council positions that prioritise internal security and migration control.

AI Act: The General Purpose AI (GPAI) model governance secondary legislation, including codes of practice for foundation models, is under active LIBE scrutiny. The committee's Fundamental Rights perspective — focusing on prohibitions on biometric surveillance, emotion recognition, and social scoring — creates flashpoints with ITRE's innovation-focused approach.

Rule of law: LIBE's ongoing monitoring of Hungary, Poland, and now Slovakia under Article 7 TEU proceedings keeps the committee in regular conflict with the Council. The EP's resolutions on rule of law carry political weight even without legally binding force.

### 🟡 AFET — Foreign Affairs
*WEP: Almost certain (90%+) that AFET is engaged in Defence White Paper oversight*
*Admiralty: B2 — Reliable source; information incomplete*

The EU Defence White Paper (released February 2026) transformed AFET's legislative agenda. The committee is now the principal parliamentary oversight body for the "ReArm Europe / SAFE" defence spending instrument, the European Defence Industrial Strategy (EDIS), and bilateral defence cooperation agreements with third countries.

Ukraine remains the central geopolitical issue, with AFET's Ukraine sub-committee meeting almost weekly. The reconstruction financing framework, expected disbursements from the €50bn facility, and accountability mechanisms are all under committee scrutiny.

Trans-Atlantic relations: AFET is monitoring the EU-US relationship following renewed trade tensions. The committee's role in scrutinising trade and technology partnerships (overlapping with INTA) creates coordination demands.

### 🟣 AFCO — Constitutional Affairs
*WEP: Likely (60–75%) that AFCO is working on electoral law harmonisation*
*Admiralty: B3 — Fairly reliable; possibly incomplete*

AFCO's document production appears high based on the direct endpoint data (30+ AFCO committee opinions visible in the metadata). This is consistent with AFCO's mandate covering the EP's internal rules, electoral law, and the institutional architecture of the EU — all areas of active legislative development in EP10.

The electoral law reform file — harmonising voter registration, candidate eligibility, and campaign financing rules across member states — has been a long-standing AFCO priority. Progress has been slower than initially hoped due to Council resistance (member states guard electoral prerogatives jealously). AFCO rapporteurs are in continuous shuttle diplomacy with Council working groups.

## Cross-Committee Dynamics

The following cross-committee dynamics are analytically significant for the week of 13–20 May 2026:

**Omnibus Package coordination**: ENVI (lead), ECON, JURI, ITRE, AGRI are all issuing opinions on the Omnibus Simplification Package within a compressed timeline. This multi-committee legislative sprint is creating coordination pressure, with potential for divergent committee positions that the Conference of Committee Chairs will need to manage.

**Defence-trade nexus**: AFET and INTA are increasingly co-ordinating on the EU's "geo-economic" security tools — export controls, foreign subsidies regulation, foreign direct investment screening, and the Anti-Coercion Instrument. This reflects the EU's evolution toward using economic tools for geopolitical purposes.

**AI governance**: LIBE (fundamental rights), ITRE (innovation), JURI (liability), and IMCO (consumer protection) all have jurisdictional stakes in the AI Act's implementation. Inter-committee coordination at the technical level is intensive.

## Political Group Dynamics in Committees

The EP10 political group composition (approximately: EPP 188, S&D 136, Patriots for Europe 84, ECR 78, Renew 77, Greens/EFA 53, ESN 25, Left 46, NI ~13) creates distinctive committee dynamics:

- **EPP-centred majority**: EPP can form majorities with either Patriots/ECR (right bloc) or with Renew/S&D (centrist majority) depending on the dossier. Committee rapporteurships are distributed accordingly.
- **Green Deal dividing line**: The most consistent cleavage in EP10 committees is between pro-Green-Deal coalitions (S&D + Greens + Left + some Renew) and anti-simplification coalitions (EPP + Patriots + ECR + some Renew).
- **Trade consensus**: Unusual cross-group consensus on trade defence instruments (anti-dumping, anti-subsidy) and on Ukraine support.
